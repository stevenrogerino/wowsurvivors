The Ember Watch

Thirty minutes until dawn

Ten survivors, five battlefields, twenty bosses, and the light that holds the dark back. You only move — everything you carry fights on its own. This is the guide to everyone you can become, everything that hunts you, and how it all fits together.

Chapter One

The vigil, in the game's own words

The prologue never explains what ember does — you already feel that, gem by gem — it explains what ember is. Alongside it, the loop that repeats for thirty minutes straight.

Every night, the dark comes up out of the ground.

It has taken every watch before this one.

What holds it back is ember — and ember will not burn on its own.

It sleeps in the stones the dead leave behind.

Break the stone. Take the light out of it. While you carry it, you burn.

They can smell a light from a long way off.

You will not kill your way out of this. Nobody ever has.

You will out-burn it, and you will keep moving.

Hold until the light comes back.

Your weapons fire themselves

You never press an attack button. Everything you carry swings, casts and reloads on its own timer, at whatever is nearest. Your whole job is where you stand.

Walk over the gems

Everything you kill drops experience. Level up and choose: a new weapon, a rank on one you carry, or a passive.

Six weapons, and no more

Rank one to 8 and learn its paired passive to evolve it into something far stronger.

Two evolved weapons can become one

Some pairs merge into a single greater weapon and hand the slot back.

Thirty minutes is the win

Bosses arrive on a schedule and the horde never stops thickening. Survive to 30:00 and the battlefield is yours — though Death itself turns up at exactly that moment.

Gold outlives the run

Keep every coin whether you win, die, or walk away. Spend it with the Trainer on lessons that apply to every run after.

Chapter Two

What the ember does to you

Every survivor climbs the same six-tier ladder — nobody earns a second class's signature, they just catch more light. It is why any two survivors, at any two ranks, never look like the same person: the ladder is shared, but the class underneath it never is.

  1. The hem takes light — embers start lifting off the survivor's own clothing.
  2. The shoulders build — a mantle of ember-lit plate grows in.
  3. The cloak lengthens — and frays into sparks at its edge.
  4. A crown of embers — the tier that reads clearly from across the field.
  5. Burning pinions — off the shoulders. Past the crown, the ember stops being cloth it was given and starts being something it does to the person wearing it.
  6. The brand — the ember itself, showing straight through the chest.
Chapter Three

Ten survivors

Two are waiting for you from the first run. The other eight are earned — five by a single feat, three by living through something the game only half-warns you about.

Chapter Four

The bestiary

Every rank-and-file horde, by the ground it walks. Gold-rimmed tiles are elites — bigger, named, and guaranteed to drop a chest.

Chapter Five

Twenty bosses

Each announces itself, then cycles attack patterns on a timer — a volley, a full ring, a charge, or a summon of its own kind. Two are not on any map's schedule at all.

Chapter Six

Six battlefields

Each is a complete thirty-minute campaign with its own horde, its own five bosses on a schedule, and its own reason the difficulty and the gold both climb together.
